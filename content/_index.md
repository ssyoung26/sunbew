**About Me**

I’m a first-year Ph.D. student in the MIT Chemistry and Chemical Biology program. My research is supported by the **Quad Fellowship**.

Before MIT, I completed my B.S. (Chemistry) and M.S. (Computer Science)  at **Stanford University** and the **Arc Institute**, advised by Professor Lingyin Li. I also spent a year at **bitBiome**, a synthetic biology startup, leveraging statistical modeling methods to discover and engineer enzymes.


**Research Interests**

I aim develop computational frameworks for spatial perturbation modeling—methods that integrate single-cell, spatial, and perturbation data to uncover how cellular neighborhoods respond to genetic or chemical interventions. Broadly, my work focuses on building technologies that accelerate the discovery and design of new therapeutics, bridging molecular insight and spatial and temporal contexts to advance our understanding of human health and disease.
