**About Me**

I’m a first-year Ph.D. student in the MIT Chemistry and Chemical Biology program, advised by **Alex Shalek**. My research is supported by the **Quad Fellowship**.

Before MIT, I completed my B.S. (Chemistry) and M.S. (Computer Science)  at **Stanford University** and the **Arc Institute**, advised by Professor Lingyin Li. I also spent a year at **bitBiome**, a synthetic biology startup, leveraging statistical modeling methods to discover and engineer enzymes.


**Research Interests**

My research interests focus on protein engineering, systems immunology, and how protein-protein interactions shape downstream immune transcriptional programs. I am also interested in leveraging AI to maximize information gained through wet lab experiments and propose new experiments in better informed ways. I am interested in combining computational and experimental approaches, including protein language models, uncertainty-aware inference, and high-throughput single-cell RNA-seq. I also work on methods integrating spatial and multiomics to study how cell–cell communication dictate how cell microenironment gives rise to tissue functions.
