**About Me**

I’m a first-year Ph.D. student in the MIT Chemistry and Chemical Biology program, advised by **Alex Shalek**. My research is supported by the **Quad Fellowship**.

Before MIT, I completed my B.S. (Chemistry) and M.S. (Computer Science)  at **Stanford University** and the **Arc Institute**, advised by Professor Lingyin Li. I also spent a year at **bitBiome**, a synthetic biology startup, leveraging statistical modeling methods to discover and engineer enzymes.


**Research Interests**

My research interests focus on cytokine engineering and immune signaling, specifically developing predictive and generative frameworks that connect protein design to functional immune cell phenotypes. I am interested in understanding how small changes in cytokine sequence and structure lead to large and sometimes unexpected effects on immune cell behavior. My work aims to bridge molecular protein engineering with systems-level immunology by studying how cytokine–receptor interactions shape downstream transcriptional programs and immune cell state transitions. I am interested in combining computational and experimental approaches, including protein language models and high-throughput single-cell multiomics. I'm also excited about methods integrating spatial and multimodal approaches to study how cytokine signaling shapes cell–cell communication within tissues.
